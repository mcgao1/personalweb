# World Rabies Day Awareness Campaign

## One-line summary
Gao co-planned and organized a World Rabies Day ("One Health, Zero Deaths") outreach campaign for OneHealthClub — free pet clinics in two Nanjing communities, an awareness lecture, and publicity materials — serving as both a core organizer and the club's publicity lead.

## Background
- **Activity**: 狂犬病日主题活动 (World Rabies Day themed campaign), run under the "One Health" banner (World Rabies Day is officially September 28; the campaign theme banner reads "One Health, Zero Deaths").
- **When**: 2022.7–2022.9 (planning through execution, culminating around World Rabies Day, Sept 28).
- **Organizers**: Hosted by 南京农业大学动物医学院 (NAU College of Veterinary Medicine), the "一带一路" International Innovation Institute for Cross-border Animal Disease Diagnosis and Immunity, and 南京农业大学万物健康国际交流协会 (OneHealthClub); the lecture/sharing session ("终结狂犬病：合作与免疫" — "Ending Rabies: Cooperation and Immunity") was held in 教四楼报告厅 at 18:30 on Sept 28, with an online option.
- **Locations for community clinics**: 玄武区红山路十字街 and 银城东苑 communities in Nanjing.

## What she did / what happened
Per her own account, Gao acted as one of the **planners and organizers** of the campaign, running both the offline volunteer activities and the online lecture. As the club's **publicity lead (宣传部长)**, she wrote and published multiple WeChat posts/articles promoting the event.

Concretely:
- Organized free veterinary clinics (义诊) for local residents' pets in two Nanjing communities (红山路十字街 and 银城东苑), where veterinary staff/students examined pets ("毛孩子") and answered owners' questions; a research/experimental dog that had previously contributed to the veterinary hospital's research also attended.
- Produced the outreach pamphlet and promotional materials for the pet clinic and campaign.
- For the rabies day awareness lecture, she was responsible for the **poster design** and overall event coordination/logistics.

## Result / outcome
The event ran as a multi-part campaign combining grassroots community outreach (free clinics) with an academic-style public lecture, reflecting a One Health framing (linking animal, human, and environmental health). Visual evidence (group photos, poster, pamphlet) confirms the event took place with visible institutional backing (vet school, international cooperation office) and a sizeable group of student volunteers/staff.
