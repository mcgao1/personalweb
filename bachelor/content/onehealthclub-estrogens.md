# UNDISIM: Dismantling the Stigmatization of "Environmental Estrogen"

## One-line summary
As team leader of "UNDISIM," Manchen Gao led a five-person team through the 2023 One Health Lunch Time Challenge (UC Davis / Nanjing Agricultural University), researching gender bias in environmental-toxicology studies of estrogen and winning First Place.

## Background
The **One Health Lunch Time Challenge** is a competition co-sponsored by the University of California, Davis (Western Institute for Food Safety and Security, WIFSS) and Nanjing Agricultural University's Office of International Relations. In 2023, Manchen — a student in Nanjing Agricultural University's Ecology program (生态201) — formed and led a five-member interdisciplinary team named **UNDISIM**. The name is explained in the team's own materials as being built from three negating prefixes ("un-/dis-/im-"), chosen deliberately to signal the team's goal: to break down ("un-do") the bias and misunderstanding ("dis-") surrounding estrogen.

Team members, as listed in the deliverables: Manchen Gao (高曼宸, Ecology, team leader), Wan Peng (彭婉, Veterinary Medicine), Qiyun Hu (胡琪芸, Veterinary Medicine), Anqi Liu (刘安琪, Animal Experimentation), and Zhuanghuan Yin (尹庄桓, Environmental Engineering).

The project's motivating observation, in the team's own words: in recent years, reporting on the spread of "environmental estrogens" (EDCs — endocrine-disrupting chemicals) and associated "feminization" has increased, but much of the underlying toxicological research narrowly focuses on gonads and reproduction in animals and humans. The team argued that this narrow focus (a) limits the scientific value of the research itself and (b) feeds public misunderstanding, reinforcing gender stereotypes and a social stigma around estrogen and "feminization." Their stated goal was to approach the topic from a One Health perspective — broadening the frame to include mental health alongside physical/biological health — in order to produce a more comprehensive, objective view and push back on the stigmatization of estrogen, in service of more inclusive social attitudes toward gender.

## What she did
As team leader, Manchen guided UNDISIM through a full research-and-outreach cycle over the course of the challenge, which culminated on June 1, 2023.

**Literature review and problem framing.** The team surveyed toxicological literature on endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) and estrogen, and identified what they describe as a recurring flaw: many studies (they cite specific reviews, e.g. on EDC exposure and female reproductive potential, EDCs and breast cancer, EDC exposure and fecundity) focus overwhelmingly on reproductive organs, gonads, and fertility outcomes, largely extrapolating from these narrow endpoints rather than building an evidence base from broader, real-world (wild-population) observation. The team's own final presentation frames this as a case in which toxicology "limits its own contribution to fundamental biological research while also distracting from pressing environmental justice needs" — narrow research questions produce sensationalized, decontextualized public narratives (e.g., panic about soy/soybean milk "causing feminization," fears of "chemical castration," mockery of men perceived as "feminized").

**Expert consultation.** The team identified and reached out to Dr. Melina Packer, a scholar whose published work (co-authored with Max R. Lambert), "What's Gender Got to Do with It? Dismantling the Human Hierarchies in Evolutionary Biology and Environmental Toxicology for Scientific and Social Progress," directly critiques the same pattern the students had identified in the toxicology literature. Manchen and her team emailed Dr. Packer with questions after reading her article, then held a live online video meeting with her (on May 19, per the team's materials) to discuss the current state of estrogen toxicology research and the relationship between estrogen and mental health under a "One Health" framing. This exchange is also referenced in Manchen's CV as her having been supervised/mentored by Dr. Packer (University of Wisconsin) during May–June 2023.

**Public engagement and original outreach materials produced by the team:**
- **A public survey** on estrogen and gender equality, which collected responses from just over 100 people (predominantly 19–23-year-old university students). Key findings the team reported: fewer than half of respondents had previously heard of "environmental estrogen" as a concept, yet a large majority expressed strong interest in gender-related topics; 80% agreed there is a trend of men "gradually feminizing," while 65% said they had personal views on solving gender-stereotype problems (vs. ~35% with no real understanding); most respondents' knowledge of estrogen effects skewed toward folk associations (obesity, "feminization," tumors) rather than accurate biological understanding.
- **Original public-education articles** critiquing the state of environmental-estrogen reporting, published on WeChat ("公众号") and QQ Zone.
- **Two original science-communication videos** made for and posted to Bilibili. The first (titled, provocatively, "the terrifying environmental estrogen" 全英科普视频/"[全英科普视频] the terrifying environmental estrogen") covered the definition, sources, transfer mechanisms, and pathways of EDCs, deliberately opening with alarming "hooks" before walking the topic back toward a more measured view — mirroring the team's mid-term presentation content. The second video (in progress/completed per the team's own tracking) focused on their final-round conclusions and argued for more critical scrutiny of current estrogen-toxicology research practice.
- **An in-person campus outreach event** at Nanjing Agricultural University, including an educational poster display ("Critical thinking of EDC") and a large public message board inviting passersby to write free-form comments on gender, "feminization," and related topics. Photos show students engaging with a poster board and the message board filling up with comments (in Chinese, English, and even some Japanese) including phrases like "Protect diversity," "Think critically," "Mental Health," and "Love is Love" — the team characterizes the response as showing that the large majority of students supported an objective, scientific view of estrogen/feminization and favored abandoning gender stereotypes.

**Final synthesis.** The team compiled their research, survey data, and outreach results into a final slide presentation ("UNDISIM — final," titled "Dismantling the Stigmatization in 'Environmental Estrogen'"), structured around: (1) reviewing the social phenomenon, (2) presenting evidence of the toxicological research's narrow framing, (3) addressing public misunderstanding, and (4) connecting estrogen/EDC science to "mental One Health." The deck closes by arguing for a genuinely multidisciplinary One Health approach — one that folds in the diversity of human society, of species, and of environments, and that treats an "inclusive, open mindset" as itself a component of health ("Mental One Health").

## Result / outcome
UNDISIM won **First Place** in the 2023 One Health Lunch Time Challenge. The certificate of achievement, dated June 1, 2023, is issued to "Team Leader Manchen Gao" for "completing the group project and successful final presentation for Team UNDISIM," co-signed by E. Robert Atwill (Director, WIFSS, UC Davis) and Bennie I. Osburn (Director for Outreach and Training, WIFSS), with an official Nanjing Agricultural University Office of International Relations seal. This matches and confirms the "won the One Health Lunch-Time Challenge" line already on her CV.
