# Course 4880: Global Food-Energy-Water Nexus Debate

## One-line summary
A joint international course (Cornell University, Tata Institute of Social Sciences, Nanjing Agricultural University, China Agricultural University) exploring global sustainability through food, energy and water; Manchen argued the Pro side in a debate on U.S. transportation electrification policy.

## Background
"4880" was a cross-institutional course taught jointly by four universities across three countries: Cornell University (USA), the Tata Institute of Social Sciences / TISS (India), and Nanjing Agricultural University and China Agricultural University (China). More than ten rotating professors contributed lectures, each speaking from their own field — agricultural economics, bioenergy, food/nutrition science, animal science — while the course as a whole used the lens of food, energy, and water to get students from all three countries discussing global sustainable development together. Manchen took the course as part of her undergraduate studies and describes it as sitting at "a nexus of humanities, science, and fun."

The one concrete graded/structured activity documented from the course is a debate exercise: "Debate 1: Should the US government make policies and provide subsidies to fully electrify the country's transportation systems?" Her team — Danny Hogg, Trevor Wise, Aimée Eicher, Adam Qi, Piyush Perfect, Radhika Singh, and Manchen Gao (a mixed international team, consistent with the course's multi-university format) — was assigned **Team Pro**, arguing that yes, the U.S. government should adopt such policies as soon as possible.

## What she did
Manchen participated as a member of the multinational student team assigned to argue the "Pro" position in the transportation-electrification debate, working alongside classmates from the U.S. and India as well as fellow Chinese students. The debate format asked each team to build a case around a real policy question at the intersection of energy, environment, and government intervention — in this case, whether federal policy and subsidies should be used to push the U.S. transportation system toward full electrification.

Beyond the single debate, she engaged with the course's broader model of rotating expert lectures spanning agricultural economics, bioenergy, food/nutrition science, and animal science, framed around the global food-energy-water nexus. In her own reflection on the course (from her course-completion write-up), she notes that she did not walk away with a fixed body of specialized knowledge the way she might from a typical major-specific class; instead, the value was in the exposure — discussing an open-ended question with peers from very different cultural and academic backgrounds, and being pushed to think about how food, energy, and animal systems are all interconnected, and how every human decision in these domains carries consequences that must be weighed carefully.

## Result / outcome
No grade, score, or debate "win/loss" is recorded in the source material — the debate slide documents the topic, team assignment, and roster but not an outcome. In her own reflection, Manchen credits this course with broadening her perspective and states that it may have been where she first laid the groundwork for her later interest in studying sustainable development.
